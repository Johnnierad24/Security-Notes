# My Personal Cybersecurity Code of Conduct

**Author:** John
**Date:** November 2026
**Status:** Active. I will review this every six months and whenever my skills or role change.

## Why I wrote this

Cybersecurity skills can protect people or harm them. The same knowledge is used by defenders and by criminals. What separates them is **permission, intent and responsibility**. This code is my promise to myself, and to anyone who works with me, about how I will use what I learn.

## My principles

1. **Permission first.** I only test systems I own or have **written permission** to test. "I was only testing" is not a defense. Without permission, it is unauthorized access.
2. **Stay in scope.** If a system, network or technique is outside the agreed scope, I do not touch it, even if it looks vulnerable or easy.
3. **Do no harm.** I avoid actions that could damage systems, delete data, interrupt services or expose people's information. I choose the least risky way to prove a point.
4. **Protect privacy.** I treat any personal data I see as confidential. I collect only what I need, store it safely, and delete it when I no longer need it.
5. **Be honest.** I report what I actually found, including my mistakes. I do not exaggerate risks, fabricate results or claim skills I do not have.
6. **Give credit.** I credit the people whose work and tools I use. I write my own reports and write-ups in my own words.
7. **Keep learning.** I stay within my skill level, ask for help when unsure, and practise in safe environments before real ones.
8. **Share knowledge responsibly.** I teach others to defend, not to harm. I do not publish working attacks against live systems.

## Where I practise

I practise only in places built for it:

- My own isolated lab (Kali Linux and practice machines in VirtualBox, on a private network)
- Learning platforms and CTF sites, following each platform's rules (for example HackerDNA, OverTheWire, TryHackMe, PortSwigger Web Security Academy, picoCTF, Hack The Box)
- Systems owned by someone who has given me written permission

I will **not** practise on my university's systems, my neighbours' Wi-Fi, businesses, government sites or any real person's accounts or devices without written permission.

## What I will do

- Read and follow the rules of every platform and every test
- Keep my lab isolated from real networks and keep vulnerable machines off the internet
- Keep notes of what I did, when and why
- Keep my own accounts secure (strong unique passwords, MFA)
- Verify information before trusting it, including answers from AI tools
- Report mistakes quickly and help fix them

## What I will not do

- Access, scan or attack any system without permission
- Steal, sell, leak or misuse data, passwords or credentials
- Use my skills for fraud, harassment, stalking, extortion or revenge
- Cause denial of service against any real service
- Install or run malware outside an isolated lab
- Share flags, answers or exploits in ways that break a competition's or platform's rules
- Paste confidential, personal or client data into AI tools
- Help someone else do any of the above

## If I find a vulnerability by accident

If I stumble on a weakness in a real system, such as an exposed file, a login bug or a leaked key:

1. **Stop.** I do not look around, test further, download data or try to "prove" it.
2. **Write down** only the minimum: what I saw, where, when and how I came across it. I do not keep any personal data I may have seen.
3. **Do not share it publicly** (no social media, no group chats) and do not boast about it.
4. **Report it privately** to the owner, using their security contact or a published responsible disclosure policy (often `security@` the domain, or `/.well-known/security.txt`). If there is no clear contact, I will ask a trusted lecturer or mentor how to report it safely.
5. **Do not demand payment** or set conditions. I describe the issue, offer help to understand it, and give the owner reasonable time to fix it.
6. **Learn from it** and write what I would have done differently.

## The law

I will follow the laws that apply to me, including Kenya's **Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018** and **Data Protection Act, 2019**, and the laws of any country where my work is used. I understand that good intentions do not remove legal responsibility, and that when I am unsure about the law I should ask a qualified person before acting.

## Using AI tools

- I try the problem myself first, then use AI to explain or review.
- I verify important AI answers in documentation or in my lab.
- I never give AI tools passwords, keys, personal data or confidential material.
- I use AI help only on systems I have permission to test.

## My commitment

I will use my skills to make people and systems safer. When I am unsure whether something is right, I will pause, ask and choose the safer option.
